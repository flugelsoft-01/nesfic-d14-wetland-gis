How to Use JalDrishti Assam — User Tutorial

Quick-start guide for ASSAC, Fisheries, Irrigation & District Officers

What Is This Application About?

Assam's wetlands (beels, oxbow lakes, and floodplain marshes) expand dramatically during the monsoon and shrink in winter. Because monsoon clouds block regular camera satellites, JalDrishti Assam combines cloud-penetrating radar satellites (Sentinel-1) with optical satellites (Sentinel-2) to give departments a year-round map of permanent vs. seasonal water, early alerts for shrinkage or water hyacinth blooms, and a mobile tool for field verification.

5-Step Walkthrough

1. Switch Seasons: Use the Pre-Monsoon, Monsoon (SAR), Post-Monsoon, and Winter buttons above the map to see how each wetland's water boundary expands and contracts.
2. Filter & Inspect Wetlands: Filter by District or Sector (Fisheries / Irrigation), or click any polygon on the map or row in the inventory to view its permanent core (dashed blue), seasonal spread, and water-quality indices.
3. Review Change Alerts: Open the Change Alerts tab to see wetlands flagged for abnormal shrinkage, water hyacinth overgrowth, or suspected encroachment. Click Locate to zoom to it or Dispatch to assign a field check.
4. Ground-Truth via Field PWA: Open the Field Verification PWA tab on a phone or browser, lock GPS coordinates, record water level, hyacinth cover, and fisheries/irrigation status, and submit to update the central audit log.
5. Export GIS Data: Click OGC / Export Data in the top-right to download GeoJSON polygons for QGIS/ArcGIS, CSV statistics for reports, or connect directly via the OGC WFS endpoint.

Technical Architecture, Formulas & Glossary

Plain-language guide to the satellite science & engineering behind JalDrishti Assam

1. Technology Stack & Engineering

All-Weather Radar + Optical Fusion

Uses Sentinel-1 SAR (radar that sees through monsoon clouds) and Sentinel-2 (multi-spectral camera) at 10m resolution to map water every 6–12 days.

Edge Web GIS & Open Standards

Powered by Cloudflare Pages & Workers (and Python FastAPI), rendering interactive vector polygons with MapLibre GL JS and exporting OGC WFS/GeoJSON.

2. Key Formulas Explained Simply

NDWI (Normalized Difference Water Index) = (Green − NIR) / (Green + NIR)

Water absorbs Near-Infrared (NIR) light while plants and soil reflect it. A positive NDWI score (above 0.2) tells us a pixel is open water rather than land.

MNDWI (Modified NDWI) = (Green − SWIR) / (Green + SWIR)

Replaces NIR with Short-Wave Infrared (SWIR) to cleanly separate shallow or muddy wetland edges from built-up embankments and roads.

NDTI (Turbidity / Silt Index) = (Red − Green) / (Red + Green)

Measures how muddy or sediment-rich the water is. Higher values indicate heavy monsoon siltation or runoff, useful for fisheries management.

Hydro-Period Persistence (%) = (Months with Water / 12) × 100

Counts how often a spot stays underwater all year. Above 75% = Permanent Water; 25–75% = Seasonal Wetland; below 25% = Ephemeral Floodplain.

3. Glossary of Terms

Beel
Assamese term for a natural floodplain wetland, oxbow lake, or marsh used for fishing and irrigation.
SAR Backscatter (dB)
Radar echo strength. Calm water acts like a mirror and bounces radar away, giving very low readings (around -18 to -21 dB).
Water Hyacinth Bloom
Fast-growing floating weed that covers open water, blocks sunlight, and reduces fish oxygen levels.
GeoJSON & OGC WFS
Open, standard map file/web formats so ASSAC officers can open JalDrishti layers directly inside QGIS or ArcGIS.
Irrigation Command Area (ha)
Total farmland area (in hectares) that can be watered from a wetland's intake or canal system.
ASSAC
Assam State Space Application Centre, the nodal state agency for remote sensing and GIS mapping in Assam.
